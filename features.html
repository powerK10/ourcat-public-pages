What it actually does

Here’s the app as it is at the moment, shown with Miso, a made-up cat, and Sam, a made-up partner. It’s free, and Android and iPhone/iPad invitations are available. The screenshots show the Android app.

Request an invitation ↗How it works
At a glance
  • Shared log
  • Reminders
  • Vet numbers
  • Sitter access
  • History and CSV
  • More than one cat

  1. Morning

    Sam feeds Miso before work and logs it. Later on you open Today, see breakfast was 40 g and that Miso finished it all, so Miso doesn’t get fed twice.

  2. Around lunchtime

    The water’s gone late. Reminders lists what’s due today and whoever gets to it taps Done. Android phone alerts depend on your role, the test version and your notification settings. iPhone/iPad alerts are still being tested. Keep your usual reminders for anything like medicine.

  3. When something looks off

    Care info is a cheat sheet for the cat. It has the emergency vet’s and usual vet’s numbers, and you just tap one to ring. This is the bit I wished I’d had when Tokyo had an accident.

  4. Friday, going away

    A friend is feeding Miso for the weekend. You give them access for three days, a week or a month. They can log what they do and see the routine but can’t change your settings, and when the time’s up their access ends by itself.

  5. Back home

    History shows who did what on any day. If someone forgot to log something you can add it after, and you can save a CSV of a date range if the vet asks.

  6. More than one cat

    Got two? Tap the name at the top to switch. You can have up to three cats in a household for now.

What it can’t do yet

It’s a test version. It keeps a record of care and that’s it. It won’t give vet advice or tell you if something is wrong. A household can have up to three cats, you plus two other people, and one sitter at a time. Each person needs their own eligible device and invitation. It’s made for a cat that more than one person looks after, like a couple at home or an owner and their regular sitter.
